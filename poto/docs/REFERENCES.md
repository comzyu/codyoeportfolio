## Design quirks Cody likes (read from the board, 16 Nov 2026)

Use these as defaults. Each is tied to the pins it came from.

1. **Things you keep, shown as objects in a container.** Spines on a shelf with one pulled forward (#0), pages fanned round an open one (#8), tabbed dividers (#12), canisters on shelves (#20), a photo disc (#5). Lists become shelves, stacks, boxes or wheels. The item you're on comes forward; the rest stay visible edge-on.
2. **Photos inside calendars.** Each day is a photo, not a dot (#4 "july recap", #15 ring-bound "sep.", #11 month posters). Empty days stay quiet, unfilled squares. Month names are big and lowercase or friendly; weekday labels are small.
3. **Office-supply realism.** Paper clips (#6, #14), bulldog clips, tape, scissors (#7), a receipt roll (#16), ring binding (#15), perforated stamp edges (#9), index tabs (#12), folders (#3). Used once or twice per screen as a single real detail, not everywhere.
4. **Handwriting next to clean type.** Script annotations beside neat sans or mono (#2 "you make me guilty", #8, #16, #17, #19). Mono for data and labels, script for the human bit.
5. **Cut-out word labels on photos.** Words set on little paper slips stuck onto an image (#2). Good for captions on a print or a recap.
6. **Calm frame, loud content.** Very plain chrome (white #10, #13, #18; black #0, #3, #20) and colour comes from the photos and objects (#11, #5). Matches "white, the photos are the colour".
7. **Small mono metadata.** Dates, counts and coordinates in tiny spaced mono ("12 / 27", "17 pages", "week 46") (#3, #8, #19).
8. **A slightly raw, printed quality.** Grain, halftone, paper texture and imperfect registration (#2, #7, #16), but never on the photo itself (Cody, 16 Nov).
